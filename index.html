Bioinformatics in the Heidel lab

Software, methods, and teaching from the bioinformatics side of the Heidel lab at Hannover Medical School (MHH): genomics tools, Hi-C prediction, Galaxy, and data analysis courses.

About

This page collects the bioinformatics work in AG Heidel, part of the Department of Hematology, Hemostasis, Oncology and Cell Therapy at Hannover Medical School. The Heidel lab studies the signaling pathways and molecules required for cell competition and self-renewal in the aging hematopoietic system and in myeloid malignancies. The bioinformatics work covers analysis methods, research software, and the infrastructure around sequencing data.

Research interests

The main research interest is chromatin architecture and epigenetics: how the genome folds in 3D, and how this folding relates to histone modifications, DNA methylation, chromatin accessibility, and gene expression. A central question is how these data types can be integrated, and whether one can be predicted from the others, for example Hi-C interaction matrices from ChIP-seq signals and DNA sequence with deep learning. Such predictions could make chromatin structure accessible for cell types and patient samples where a Hi-C experiment is not feasible.

A second interest is the modernization and digitalization of clinical workflows in hematology diagnostics: replacing manual and paper-based steps with reproducible, traceable software, from sample tracking in the sequencing lab to automated analysis pipelines on Galaxy.

Both lines of work make use of AI. Deep learning is the basis of the prediction methods, and agentic AI is used as a development tool: to rewrite and speed up existing genomics software, and to build and maintain the software behind the clinical workflows.

  • Research

    Chromatin architecture software (HiCExplorer, Hi-cGAN), SampleSync for the sequencing lab, LeukoPredict for calls from raw instrument data in leukemia diagnostics, agentic AI translations of genomics tools to C++ (falcoAmadeus, vardictcpp), and Galaxy wrappers and training material.

  • Teaching

    Galaxy and Python courses and machine learning lectures in the HBRS PhD programs, lectures in the master's program Biomedical Data Science, and course material.
